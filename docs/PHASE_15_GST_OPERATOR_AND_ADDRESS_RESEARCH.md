## Direct conclusions

### Website address

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 apply broadly to goods and
services bought or sold over a digital/electronic network and to inventory and
marketplace models. Rule 4(2) requires every e-commerce entity to provide its
legal name, the principal geographic address of its headquarters and all
branches, website details, and customer-care/grievance contacts clearly,
accessibly, and prominently on its platform. The 2021 amendment narrows the
resident nodal-officer rule for specified corporate/foreign entities; it does
not remove Rule 4(2) or create a proprietorship/address exemption.

The best-supported conservative application is that GoneViral/AltCorp is within
that rule: it systematically offers a paid service through its website. Dodo's
merchant-of-record role for checkout does not create an identified exemption
from the platform operator's Rule 4(2) disclosure.

The rule does not say that the full address must be repeated in Terms, Privacy,
Refunds, paid-placement disclosures, checkout copy, every page footer, or
metadata. One dedicated operator/contact disclosure containing the address,
prominently and clearly linked from the site footer, checkout, and relevant
legal pages, is the least-duplicative implementation. The grievance-specific
Rule 4(4) separately requires the officer's name, designation, and contact
details, not a postal address. GST rules identified below do not independently
require a GSTIN or principal address on the website.

The IT Sensitive Personal Data Rules' privacy-policy contents cover collection,
purpose, disclosure, security practices, and an accessible grievance process;
they do not add a full operator postal-address field to the Privacy Policy. The
phased DPDP Act/Rules require business contact information for a responsible
person and accessible rights/grievance methods, not a residential address in
each privacy notice. The underlying public-address duty is therefore the
platform-level Consumer Rule 4(2) duty, not a reason to duplicate the home
address throughout the policy set.

The owner resolved the engineering decision by authorizing this
privacy-minimized public geographic address only for the dedicated
operator/contact disclosure:

`4th Cross Road, Noor Khan Colony, Kalaburagi, Karnataka 585104, India`

The authorization expressly excludes the residence's house/flat number,
building or premises name, nearby landmark, and any reconstructed equivalent.
Do not duplicate the authorized address in the global footer, metadata,
checkout, or unrelated pages.

Rule 4(2) does not prescribe address components or expressly approve a redacted
address. Omitting premise-level details may be challenged under a strict reading
of "principal geographic address." No lawyer approval or definitive compliance
conclusion is claimed. The owner accepts that residual compliance risk in order
to protect the residence while allowing remaining launch engineering to
continue. A future genuine non-residential headquarters and corresponding
GST/business-record amendment remains the stronger privacy-preserving option.

### GSTIN and tax-document address

CGST Rule 18 requires the registration certificate to be displayed at the
principal and every additional place of business, and the GSTIN on the name
board at the entrance to each such physical place. It does not impose a website
or policy-page GSTIN display requirement.

CGST Rule 46 requires a tax invoice issued by a registered supplier to contain
the supplier's name, address, and GSTIN, plus the other prescribed invoice
fields. Related supplier-issued receipt, refund, credit, and debit documents
also contain supplier identity/address fields. Rule 46 says "address of the
supplier" rather than expressly saying "principal place of business". The safe
operational reading is to use a genuine registered place from which the supply
is made; a made-up or mail-only address is not acceptable.

Notification 26/2022 added that a tax invoice for taxable services supplied by
or through an electronic-commerce operator to an unregistered recipient must
also contain the recipient's name, address, PIN, and State. Dodo already
requires the customer's billing address for invoicing. This recipient-address
rule does not remove Rule 46's separate supplier-identity fields and does not
make the proprietor's home a customer field.

Dodo's current documentation says it is the legal seller/merchant of record for
the customer transaction and that the customer invoice carries Dodo's tax
details. On that model, the customer-facing invoice should identify Dodo as the
supplier and need not expose AltCorp's residential address or GSTIN merely for
branding. This conclusion must be verified against the actual live agreement
and one genuine Dodo invoice before activation; the application must not issue
a competing GoneViral/AltCorp customer tax invoice.

Dodo also documents a separate reverse invoice for payouts to the business.
That provider/business document and any tax document AltCorp is required to
issue to Dodo may properly contain AltCorp's legal name, genuine registered
address, and GSTIN. It is accounting/provider evidence, not a public website or
customer-facing disclosure.

## Privacy-preserving address alternatives

A real separately occupied, leased, licensed, consented, or shared/co-working
business location can be used for GST registration when the operator has valid
possession documents and actually carries on business or keeps records there.
The GST registration form recognizes owned, leased, rented, consented, and
shared premises; GSTN's official registration FAQ specifically supports a
co-working principal place as "Shared". CBIC's 2025 instruction lists acceptable
ownership, rent/lease, consent, and supporting-premises documents.

The alternative must become the genuine headquarters/principal place, not just
a correspondence or mail-forwarding address. CGST Act section 2(85) ties a
place of business to where business is ordinarily carried on, goods/services
are supplied or received, books are kept, or business is conducted through an
agent. Rule 21 permits cancellation where business is not conducted from the
declared place.

When the actual principal or additional place changes, Rule 19 requires FORM
GST REG-14 with supporting documents within the prescribed period and officer
approval for address changes. Adding an alternate location merely as an
additional place does not by itself solve the Consumer Rules issue if the home
remains GoneViral's headquarters or a branch: Rule 4(2) calls for the
headquarters and all branches. The privacy-preserving route is therefore to
move the genuine business headquarters and amend the registration/business
records, not to add a cosmetic correspondence address.
